# Austin's writing voice

Derived from the 2017–2020 posts and reviewed by Austin (2026-10-12). Update it as
polishing sessions show what he keeps, cuts, and rewrites.

## The goal
Easy-to-follow guides and short summaries that respect the reader's time. Most of the
internet (and most how-to videos) is too long; these posts should be the opposite. When
in doubt, cut.

## All posts
- **First person, conversational.** Write like explaining to a friend or a labmate who is
  smart but wasn't there: "My MacBook was getting slow so…", "Here is how I did it:".
- **One or two lines of why, then go.** Say what prompted it; no general preamble.
- **Honest about what didn't work.** Failed attempts, broken bolts, and "she may just be
  being nice" stay in. Uncertainty is stated plainly, not hedged: "Script could use some
  updates."
- **Casual, but edited.** Keep his plain, slightly rough phrasing; don't smooth it into
  magazine prose. Do fix spelling, cut repetition, and make every sentence earn its place.
- **Light, dry humor in small doses.** Parenthetical asides, a "haha" or "Profit!" now and
  then, understatement ("Yes this is really only 6 steps."). At most a couple per post.
- **Link instead of re-explaining**, and credit where info came from.
- **Open questions are fine to leave open**: "Anyone?"

## Tech notes
- **Answer first.** Context in a line, then the steps. Someone should be able to skim it
  and do the thing.
- Numbered steps, one action per step, details as sub-bullets.
- Commands in fenced blocks, ready to copy. Note versions/OS when it matters.
- Gotchas get their own short list rather than being buried in steps.

## Project write-ups
- **Story order is fine**: goal → what I tried → what broke → fix → result. The failures
  and fixes are usually the most useful part; give them room.
- Open with one line on what it is and why, so a reader knows if it's for them.
- Let photos, drawings, and models carry detail instead of long descriptions.
- Skip blow-by-blow narration that doesn't help someone build or understand it.
- End on the result or what's next.

## Shape (all posts)
- `##` section headings that say what's in the section ("Too Much Power", "Setting up my
  environment"), not generic ones ("Introduction", "Conclusion").
- Short paragraphs. Lists when there are more than two parallel things.
- No summary recap at the end.
- Photos sit next to the text they illustrate, usually
  `{: style='width:50%; float:right'}`.

## Avoid
- Marketing or LinkedIn tone, hype adjectives ("incredible", "game-changing"), and
  "In this post I will…" openers.
- Padding: long intros, restating the title, "why this matters" sections, recaps,
  repeating the same point in different words, filler transitions ("Now that we've…").
- Over-explaining basics he'd assume the reader can Google.
- Em-dash-heavy, overly smooth prose.
- Inventing details, numbers, names, or motives that aren't in his notes.
- Copying his old typos (brake/break, below/bellow).
